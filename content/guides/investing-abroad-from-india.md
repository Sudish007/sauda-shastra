## LRS is the remittance boundary

The RBI’s [Liberalised Remittance Scheme Master Direction](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=10192) lets a resident individual remit up to {{fact:glb-lrs-limit}} in one financial year across permitted current- and capital-account purposes. Overseas portfolio investment in shares and ETFs is permitted under the FEMA overseas-investment framework. The limit is per person, not per bank or broker.

The remitter gives the authorised-dealer bank a purpose declaration, commonly Form A2, and must aggregate remittances across banks. The bank cannot lend money for a capital-account LRS remittance. Unused or realised foreign exchange must generally return to India within {{fact:glb-lrs-idle-funds-days}} days unless it is reinvested; foreign income can be retained and reinvested under the direction.

An overseas account offered to an Indian resident does not make every global product permissible. [Interactive Brokers India](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.in/en/accounts/individual-account-steps-in.php) says resident clients can trade foreign stocks, bonds and ETFs, but not overseas futures, options or margin products. Check FEMA eligibility before checking the ticker.

## TCS is a cash-flow cost, not necessarily final tax

Section 394(1), Table Sl. 7 of the [Income-tax Act 2025](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-394-6) requires the authorised dealer to collect TCS on the part of aggregate LRS remittances above {{fact:glb-tcs-threshold}}. For investment and other non-education or medical purposes, the rate is {{fact:glb-tcs-rate}}. The bank aggregates the year before applying the threshold.

:::example The threshold applies to the excess
If investment remittances in the year total ₹25,00,000, the slice above {{fact:glb-tcs-threshold}} is ₹15,00,000. TCS at {{fact:glb-tcs-rate}} is ₹3,00,000. A total remittance of ₹8,00,000 stays below the threshold and produces no TCS.
:::

TCS is reported against the remitter’s PAN and normally appears in Form 26AS or AIS. It is credited against the final Indian tax liability or refunded after the return is processed. The timing still matters: cash collected during the year cannot remain invested while it waits for credit or refund. Reconcile the bank certificate with the tax statement.

## Choose the route by legal ownership

| Route | What the investor owns | Main checks |
|---|---|---|
| Overseas broker account | Foreign shares or ETFs in overseas custody | LRS funding, product permission, statements, FX and estate situs |
| IFSCA Global Access Provider | Foreign security reached through a regulated GIFT City chain | Provider, introducing broker, custodian, tariff and investor protection |
| Fintech linked to a broker-dealer | Security held through the disclosed US or IFSC entity | Legal entity, registration, custody, withdrawal and transfer-out process |
| India-domiciled fund or FoF | Units of an Indian mutual-fund scheme | Portfolio, two layers of cost, tax classification and subscription status |

The [IFSCA Global Access framework](https://www.ifsca.gov.in/CommonDirect/ViewFile?id=21626bde60601ef44a0ed022017ceaa5&fileName=Press_Release___Revamped_Regulatory_Framework_for_Global_Access_in_the_IFSC_20250812_0842.pdf) is the current GIFT City model. [NSEIX Global Access](https://www.nseixga.com/) is one provider example. It is different from the old NSE IFSC Receipts programme.

:::warning The receipt programme is ending
[NSE IX circular NSEIFSC/TRADE/2698](https://www.nseix.com/api/content/circulars/NSEIFSC_TRADE_2698.pdf) stopped buy orders in NSE IFSC Receipts on {{date:2026-07-30}} and ends dealing on {{date:2027-02-01}}. The replacement route is IFSCA Global Access, not a renamed receipt. Existing holders must follow the circular’s sale or conversion timetable.
:::

India-domiciled international funds avoid a personal foreign brokerage account but sit under the SEBI industry limit {{fact:glb-mf-overseas-cap}}. The [SEBI mutual-fund master circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774024028162.pdf) restates that cap. A scheme can stop subscriptions when capacity is unavailable, so a standing instruction needs a current scheme notice.

## US dividends, W-8BEN and foreign tax credit

A valid W-8BEN certifies foreign status and treaty eligibility to the US withholding agent. Its normal validity is {{fact:glb-w8ben-validity}}, unless circumstances change earlier. Track the expiry; otherwise the statutory withholding can replace the treaty rate.

For a portfolio investor resident in India, US dividends are normally withheld at {{fact:glb-us-dividend-withholding-dtaa}} under Article 10 of the [India–US treaty](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/india.pdf). India taxes the **gross** dividend at the applicable slab. Article 25 permits credit for US tax on the same income, capped at the Indian tax attributable to it.

The Income Tax Department’s [Form 67 page](https://wm.incometaxindia.gov.in/web/guest/w/form-67), updated on {{date:2026-05-15}}, still cites **Rule 128 of the 1962 Rules**. The corresponding Income-tax Rules 2026 number was not fetched, so this guide keeps that dated description rather than inventing a replacement. Preserve the dividend voucher, withholding statement and conversion-rate record.

## Indian capital gains and disclosure

A foreign-listed share or ETF is long-term only after {{fact:glb-foreign-stock-ltcg-period}} because the shorter listed-security test applies to securities listed in India. Long-term gains use {{fact:glb-foreign-stock-ltcg-rate}} without indexation under s. 197; shorter holdings are taxed at the slab. Dividends are slab income with foreign-tax credit handled separately.

Acquisition cost, sale proceeds and distributions must be translated under the applicable Indian rules. Keep remittance advice, contract notes, monthly and year-end broker statements, corporate-action records and the exact withholding document. A broker dashboard’s INR display is not a tax working paper.

Schedule FA reporting should be treated generically until the current form instructions are read. An ordinarily resident taxpayer may need to report foreign shares, fund units, cash and brokerage accounts. This guide does **not** state unverified Income-tax Rules 2026 field names or a reporting-basis convention. Section 43 of the [Black Money Act](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-43-114) sets a penalty of {{fact:glb-schedule-fa-rule}} for failure to report a foreign asset or income, with its statutory small-asset carve-out. A chartered accountant should check the current return form.

## Direct US holdings create estate-tax exposure

The IRS treats shares of US companies, including US-domiciled ETFs, as US-situs property for a non-resident non-citizen estate. Filing exposure begins above {{fact:glb-us-estate-exemption-nra}}, which is not inflation-indexed. The unified rate schedule reaches {{fact:glb-us-estate-top-rate}}.

:::example A direct US ETF estate
Under the [IRS Form 706 rate table](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i706), a $500,000 US-situs estate produces tentative tax of $155,800. Subtract the $13,000 unified credit available to the ordinary non-resident estate and the result is about $142,800, or 28.6% of the holding. India has no US estate-tax treaty that raises this threshold.
:::

This is a low threshold relative to a long-term portfolio. Beneficiary nominations and broker succession procedures do not remove federal estate tax. The legal owner and fund domicile determine situs, not the exchange screen or the investor’s citizenship label in an app.

## The Ireland-domiciled UCITS alternative

An Ireland-domiciled UCITS ETF is a share of an Irish fund, not a share of a US company. For a non-US investor, that fund unit is generally outside US estate-tax situs even when the fund owns US shares. The fund itself receives US dividends at {{fact:glb-ucits-us-dividend-withholding}} under the Ireland–US treaty, versus the direct Indian-holder rate above.

An accumulating class reinvests distributions inside the fund. The Indian holder receives no annual cash dividend from the fund, but the fund-level withholding remains inside NAV and Indian capital-gains tax applies on sale. [CSPX](https://www.ishares.com/uk/individual/en/products/253743/ishares-sp-500-b-ucits-etf-acc-fund) is an Ireland-domiciled accumulating S&P 500 fund used only as a structure example; its provider TER is {{fact:glb-cspx-ter}}.

The [Irish Revenue manual](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/capital-acquisitions-tax/cat-manual/part-09-exemptions/part-9-exemptions.pdf) describes an Irish capital-acquisitions-tax exemption for qualifying investment-fund units where neither party meets the stated Irish domicile or ordinary-residence conditions. It does not remove Indian tax, fund cost, exchange spread or foreign-asset disclosure.

## The cost calculator’s two worked cases

The [US-versus-India investing-cost calculator](../../tools/us-vs-india-investing-cost/) treats the FX spread as an editable **assumption** because no primary dated bank-wide markup table was found. TCS is displayed separately as recoverable tax cash flow, not added to annual friction.

:::example Calculator case one
Remit ₹15,00,000 with a 0.50% FX-spread assumption, no fixed transfer fee and no earlier LRS remittance. Use a US TER of {{fact:glb-voo-expense-ratio}}, a 1.30% dividend-yield assumption, withholding of {{fact:glb-us-dividend-withholding-dtaa}} and an India-route TER assumption of 0.20%.

The model leaves ₹14,92,500 invested after ₹7,500 FX cost. Annual dividends are ₹19,402.50; withholding is ₹4,850.63 and US TER is ₹447.75. Total first-year US friction is ₹12,798.38, versus ₹3,000 of India-route TER, a difference of ₹9,798.38. TCS is ₹1,00,000 under the registered threshold and rate, shown separately.
:::

:::example Calculator case two
Remit ₹8,00,000 with a 1.00% FX-spread assumption and ₹500 fixed fee, keeping the same TER and yield assumptions. The amount is below {{fact:glb-tcs-threshold}}, so TCS is ₹0.

The model leaves ₹7,91,500 invested. FX cost is ₹8,000, US TER ₹237.45 and dividend withholding ₹2,572.38, making first-year US friction ₹11,309.83. India-route TER is ₹1,600, so the displayed difference is ₹9,709.83.
:::

Both cases are comparisons, not return forecasts. Replace every assumption with the bank quote, broker tariff, chosen fund document and expected holding pattern. The result omits capital gains because future returns and USD/INR are unknown.
