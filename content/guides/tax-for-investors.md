## Long-term or short-term

Everything in an investor's tax bill turns on one date test. Under s. 2(101) of the [Income-tax Act 2025](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/income-tax-act-2025) a capital asset is short-term if held for 24 months or less — but for listed securities, units of equity-oriented funds, UTI units and zero-coupon bonds the line is drawn earlier: they are long-term when held {{fact:tax-holding-period-listed}}. A Nifty 50 index fund bought on a date and sold a year and a day later is a long-term asset; sold on the anniversary itself, it is not.

The holding period runs from the date of acquisition of each unit or share, so a SIP is many small purchases with many small clocks, and a redemption is matched against them first-in, first-out. The [SIP calculator](../../tools/sip-compounding/) shows how much of a long-running SIP's value is gain rather than contribution — the part the tax applies to.

## The rates on listed equity and equity funds

The special rates apply to shares and equity-oriented fund units on which securities transaction tax was paid, and to units of business trusts. Both rates date from {{date:2024-07-23}} under the 1961 Act and were carried into the 2025 Act unchanged.

- **Short-term (s. 196):** {{fact:tax-stcg-rate}} of the gain.
- **Long-term (s. 198):** {{fact:tax-ltcg-rate}} of the gain above {{fact:tax-ltcg-exemption}} in a tax year, with no indexation. A resident individual or HUF whose other income is below the basic exemption may set the unused part of that exemption against these gains first.

The cost of acquisition is what was paid, including brokerage and stamp duty on the purchase; the STT paid is **not** deductible for capital gains (s. 76(4)). The [charges calculator](../../tools/charges/) shows what a delivery trade pays on each side.

:::example Two gains at the special rates
1. **Index-fund units held fourteen months.** Bought for ₹4,00,000, sold for ₹5,60,000: a long-term gain of ₹1,60,000. Less the {{fact:tax-ltcg-exemption}} exemption, ₹35,000 is taxable at {{fact:tax-ltcg-rate}} = ₹4,375, plus {{fact:tax-cess-rate}} cess of ₹175: **₹4,550** in all, about 2.8 per cent of the gain.
2. **Shares held six months.** A gain of ₹45,000 is short-term: {{fact:tax-stcg-rate}} = ₹9,000, plus cess ₹360, **₹9,360** — about a fifth of the gain, with no exemption to shelter it. Holding the same position past the twelve-month line would have moved it to the first row.
:::

The exemption is per tax year, not per sale, so an investor who realises long-term gains every year uses it every year; one who sells a decade of accumulation in one go uses it once. Spreading large redemptions across tax years — where the investment case allows — is the one piece of planning the exemption rewards.

## Losses: set-off and carry-forward

{{label:MECHANISM}} Capital losses stay within capital gains. A short-term capital loss can be set off against any capital gain, short or long (s. 108(2)); a long-term capital loss only against long-term gains. Either carries forward for eight tax years (s. 111), on the same terms — but only if the return claiming it is filed by the due date (s. 121). A capital loss is never set off against salary, business income or interest (s. 109). In the other direction, a non-speculative business loss such as an F&O loss can be set off against capital gains in the same year, while a speculation loss from intraday equity cannot — see [Tax for traders](../tax-for-traders-it-act-2025/).

:::example A loss in the same year
Short-term capital loss ₹30,000 on a stock sold within the year, and a long-term gain of ₹1,40,000 on fund units. The short-term loss is set off against the long-term gain first: ₹1,40,000 − ₹30,000 = ₹1,10,000, which is below the {{fact:tax-ltcg-exemption}} exemption, so the tax is **nil**. Without the set-off, ₹15,000 would have been taxable at {{fact:tax-ltcg-rate}}. The order of set-off is the taxpayer's choice within the rules, and taking the loss against the gain that would otherwise be taxed is the whole point.
:::

## Dividends

Dividends from Indian companies are taxed at slab in the shareholder's hands — there is no special rate for residents — and the paying company deducts TDS of {{fact:tax-dividend-tds-rate}} under s. 393 once an individual's dividends from it cross ten thousand rupees in the year and are paid other than in cash. The TDS is a credit against the final liability, not the final tax, and it appears in the department's annual information statement, which should be reconciled with the dividend advices before filing.

:::example A year's dividends from one company
₹16,000 of dividends in a tax year: TDS of {{fact:tax-dividend-tds-rate}} = ₹1,600 is deducted at source. The full ₹16,000 is added to total income and taxed at the investor's slab — in a twenty-per-cent slab, ₹3,200 plus cess — with the ₹1,600 credited against it. For an investor below the basic exemption the TDS is refunded through the return.
:::

## Debt funds and the "always short-term" assets

{{label:MECHANISM}} Section 76 removes the long-term treatment from three classes of asset regardless of holding period: units of a **Specified Mutual Fund** — one that invests more than sixty-five per cent in debt and money-market instruments — acquired on or after {{date:2023-04-01}}; market-linked debentures; and unlisted bonds and debentures transferred on or after {{date:2024-07-23}}. Their gains are {{fact:tax-debt-fund-rule}}. A liquid, money-market, corporate-bond or gilt fund bought since April 2023 is therefore taxed like interest when sold, however long it was held; units bought before that date keep the older treatment.

:::example A debt fund held three years
Units of a corporate-bond fund bought in 2024 and sold three years later with a gain of ₹70,000. Because s. 76 applies, the gain is short-term and taxed at slab: in the twenty-per-cent slab, ₹14,000 plus cess of ₹560 — **₹14,560**, against ₹8,750 plus cess (before the exemption) had the gain been a long-term equity gain at {{fact:tax-ltcg-rate}}. The after-tax return of a debt fund is a slab question, and investors in the top slab should compare it with the yield after tax of a fixed deposit, not with equity.
:::

Gold ETFs and international funds have their own rules; they belong to the Investing sections' guides on asset allocation and on investing abroad, which cover them as they are published.

## Rebate, surcharge and cess

Three items change the bill without changing the rates.

- **The s. 156 rebate** of up to {{fact:tax-rebate-156}} applies when total income is at most {{fact:tax-rebate-156-income-limit}}, but it is capped at the tax computed at the slab rates (s. 156(3)) — so it does **not** offset the special-rate tax on equity gains under ss. 196 and 198. An investor with a small salary and a large long-term gain still pays {{fact:tax-ltcg-rate}} on the gain above the exemption.
- **Surcharge** starts above fifty lakh rupees of total income and rises with income, but on dividends and on ss. 196–198 gains it is capped at {{fact:tax-surcharge-cg-cap}} ([Finance Act 2026](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/finance-acts), s. 3 and the First Schedule).
- **Cess** of {{fact:tax-cess-rate}} is charged on the tax plus any surcharge, including the tax on capital gains — the ₹175 and ₹360 in the examples above.

## Records and the return

- **Contract notes** for every buy and sell, which carry the dates the holding-period test needs and the costs that go into the acquisition price.
- **Demat holding and transaction statements**, and for mutual funds the **capital-gains statements** from the fund house or registrar, which already apply first-in, first-out across SIP instalments.
- **Dividend advices** and the TDS shown against them.
- The department's **annual information statement**, reconciled line by line before filing; mismatches are the usual reason for a notice.

An investor with capital gains and dividends but no business income files ITR-2 for the return due in 2026, per the department's [returns page](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/income-tax-returns); the due date for a non-audited individual is {{fact:tax-return-due-date-other}}, and a loss carried forward needs the return filed by that date. The new return forms under the Income-tax Rules 2026 for the tax year 2026-27 had not been notified on {{date:2026-11-01}}; the [What changed](../../changelog/) page records them when they are.
