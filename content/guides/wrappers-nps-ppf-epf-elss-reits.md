## Start with the wrapper, then inspect the asset

A wrapper changes access, tax, contribution rules or the way income reaches the investor. It does not turn a risky asset into a safe one. NPS and ELSS can own equity; PPF and EPF are long-horizon savings arrangements; REITs and InvITs are listed business trusts; a debt fund is a market portfolio, not a deposit.

Compare every option on four columns:

1. **What owns the money?** Government account, provident-fund trust, pension account, mutual fund or listed trust.
2. **What creates the return?** Notified interest, market securities, rent, infrastructure cash flow or debt yields.
3. **When can money leave?** Daily liquidity, lock-in, retirement conditions or scheme-specific withdrawal rules.
4. **How is each cash flow taxed?** Deduction on contribution, annual income, distribution components and gain on exit are separate questions.

A tax deduction cannot rescue a bad asset fit. Put the wrapper into the [asset-allocation plan](../asset-allocation-and-gold-after-sgb/) first, then choose the provider or scheme.

## PPF and EPF: rates with different promises

### Public Provident Fund

The National Savings Institute’s [rate table](https://www.nsiindia.gov.in/InternalPage.aspx?Id_Pk=132) and Department of Posts [SB Order 12/2026](https://utilities.cept.gov.in/dop/pdfbind.ashx?id=15533) show the current PPF rate as {{fact:inv-ppf-rate}}, unchanged for the October–December 2026 quarter. It is government-notified and compounded yearly. That is different from an equity return assumption: the rate can be reset for future quarters, but the account does not mark down every day with the market.

PPF has a long initial term, limited withdrawals and extensions under its scheme rules. The access restriction is part of the product, not an inconvenience to ignore. Use it for money that can remain locked and count the balance in the debt side of the allocation.

:::example PPF accumulation, with the assumptions visible
Contribute an amount equal to the current Schedule XV savings-deduction cap, {{fact:inv-80c-equivalent-limit}}, at the start of each year for fifteen years. If every year earned today’s {{fact:inv-ppf-rate}}, the contributions of ₹22,50,000 would grow to about ₹40,68,209. The result assumes the current rate never changes; it is a comparison case, not what the government has promised for future quarters. The [compounding calculator](../../tools/sip-compounding/) lets the rate and horizon be changed.
:::

### Employees’ Provident Fund

The EPF number needs a status label, not only a percentage. The EPFO Central Board of Trustees, at its meeting on {{date:2026-03-02}}, **recommended** {{fact:inv-epf-rate}} for FY 2025-26, according to the [PIB release](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2234502). The official government notification was not fetched for this research. The register therefore dates and labels the figure to the CBT recommendation rather than presenting press reports of ratification as primary evidence.

EPF is employment-linked and combines employee and employer flows under scheme rules. Treat the balance as retirement debt exposure, check the passbook and nomination, and read the current withdrawal rules before assuming it can fund a nearer goal. Comparing EPF’s stated rate with an equity-fund CAGR without comparing risk, liquidity and tax is meaningless.

## NPS and ELSS: equity inside a rulebook

### NPS after the standardised scheme framework

PFRDA’s [August 2026 circular](https://pfrda.org.in/documents/33652/154008/Circular+-+Standardised+framework+for+classification+and+presentation+of+Schemes+under+the+NPS.pdf) supersedes the older shorthand that “NPS equity is always capped at one number”. Under standard Active Choice in Tier I, the equity allocation can be up to {{fact:inv-nps-equity-cap}}. A separately classified Multiple Scheme Framework Category A scheme has an equity mandate reaching {{fact:inv-nps-msf-equity-cap}}. Tier II and employer/government arrangements have their own rules.

Those are ceilings, not suggested allocations. The pension account also carries exit and annuitisation rules, manager choice, asset-class limits and charges. A high-equity MSF scheme is still an equity portfolio with retirement access constraints. Choose the allocation by retirement capacity, then the scheme category.

### ELSS and the savings deduction

An Equity Linked Savings Scheme is an equity mutual fund with a statutory lock-in of {{fact:inv-elss-lock-in}} from the allotment of each purchase under [Rule 3(d)](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/equity-linked-savings-scheme-2005). A SIP therefore creates a separate release date for every instalment.

Section 123 of the Income-tax Act 2025 groups PPF, recognised provident-fund contributions, ELSS and other Schedule XV payments under a combined deduction cap of {{fact:inv-80c-equivalent-limit}}. The register labels it for the old-regime computation. Compare the complete old- and new-regime tax bills before investing for a deduction; do not multiply the cap by the marginal tax rate and call that a return.

{{label:MECHANISM}} ELSS has market risk throughout the lock-in. The lock-in can stop panic selling, but it also prevents a sale when the goal or fund choice changes. Select it as an equity fund first — benchmark, direct-plan cost, portfolio and tracking or active process — and only then count any deduction.

## REITs and InvITs: one price, several income types

A REIT owns income-producing real estate through trusts and special-purpose vehicles; an InvIT does the parallel job for infrastructure. Both trade like securities, but their distributions are not one simple dividend.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} Under the [SEBI REIT Regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/apr-2026/1776685013526.pdf), at least four-fifths of a REIT’s assets are in completed, rent- or income-generating property, and REIT special-purpose vehicles distribute at least {{fact:inv-reit-distribution-min}} of net distributable cash flow to the trust. The trading lot is a single unit. The InvIT regulations were not separately fetched for this guide, so do not infer every asset test from the REIT text; read the current InvIT offer document.

Section 223 of the [Income-tax Act 2025](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-223-74) makes business-trust distributions retain their character in the unit holder’s hands. One payment can contain:

- interest, generally taxed as interest at slab;
- rent, where the trust structure passes it through;
- dividend, whose treatment depends on the special-purpose vehicle’s tax position;
- repayment or return-of-capital components, which may reduce cost or be taxed under the applicable rule.

The annual tax statement and distribution notice supply the split. The cash credited by the broker is not enough to file the return. On sale, listed-unit capital-gains rules apply separately from the distributions.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Yield is not a fixed-deposit rate. Occupancy, rent escalation, refinancing, project traffic, regulation and interest rates change the trust’s cash flow and market price. Compare net asset value, leverage, debt maturity, sponsor transactions and distribution coverage, not only the trailing yield.

## Debt funds after the 2023 change

Section 76 of the [Income-tax Act 2025](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-76-102) applies to units of a Specified Mutual Fund acquired on or after {{date:2023-04-01}}. The rule in the register is {{fact:tax-debt-fund-rule}}. The definition uses the annual average of daily holdings: more than sixty-five per cent in debt and money-market instruments, or a fund-of-funds meeting the linked test.

That removes the old reason to hold a qualifying debt fund merely for long-term indexation. It does not make every debt fund a poor product. Liquidity, duration control, diversification and mark-to-market access can still fit a goal. Compare the post-tax yield with a deposit, bond, PPF or EPF under the investor’s slab, and include credit and interest-rate risk.

:::example Debt-fund and equity-gain tax, simplified
Assume a ₹1,00,000 gain on a qualifying debt fund bought after the rule date. For an investor in the highest displayed slab, {{fact:tax-slab-7}}, tax is ₹30,000 and {{fact:tax-cess-rate}} takes the total to ₹31,200. The same gain qualifying for the listed-equity long-term rate would produce ₹12,500 before cess and ₹13,000 after cess, assuming the annual exemption is already used. The comparison ignores surcharge and loss set-off; [Tax for investors](../tax-for-investors/) handles those layers.
:::

Hybrid and gold funds need classification rather than a name-based guess. A gold ETF follows the listed-gold rule in the [verified register](../../facts/#fact-inv-gold-etf-tax-rule); an international or debt-heavy fund may fall under a different holding test. Read the scheme portfolio and the tax section of the current document.

## Put the wrappers in one comparison

| Wrapper or asset | Return source | Access | Main tax question | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PPF | Government-notified interest | Long lock-in and scheme withdrawals | Whether the Schedule XV deduction helps the chosen regime | Future rates reset |
| EPF | EPFO-declared annual interest | Employment and retirement rules | Contribution, withdrawal and service history | Record and access constraints |
| NPS | Chosen market portfolio | Pension exit rules | Contribution and exit treatment | Market risk plus lock-in |
| ELSS | Equity portfolio | Each allotment locked for {{fact:inv-elss-lock-in}} | Combined Schedule XV cap | Equity drawdown and fund selection |
| REIT / InvIT | Rent, interest and operating cash flow | Exchange liquidity | Character of each distribution plus sale gain | Leverage, occupancy or project cash flow |
| Specified debt fund | Debt interest and price movement | Fund redemption rules | Gain is {{fact:tax-debt-fund-rule}} | Duration, credit and tax at slab |

A wrapper earns a place when its access rule matches the goal, its underlying asset matches the allocation, and its after-tax outcome beats a simpler alternative. The order matters. Starting with a deduction or headline yield invites the investor to buy a lock-in first and discover the asset later.

- {{label:EVIDENCE}} PPF’s current rate comes from the government table and circular; the EPF figure is explicitly the CBT recommendation; NPS limits come from the 2026 PFRDA framework; ELSS lock-in, the savings cap, REIT distributions and debt-fund tax come from primary rules.
- {{label:MECHANISM}} Counting locked accounts in allocation, separating a business-trust distribution and comparing after-tax debt yields are operating methods, not return forecasts.
- {{label:LORE}} Choosing a wrapper because its latest declared rate is highest, or treating a REIT yield as fixed: neither claim follows from the cited rules.
