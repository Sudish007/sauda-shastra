## Allocation comes before selection

Asset allocation answers a harder question than “which fund”: how much loss can the plan absorb without missing the goal or forcing a sale? Equity supplies uncertain growth, debt supplies a more stable spending reserve, and gold supplies a different source of price movement and crisis liquidity. None is a complete portfolio by itself.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Start with the liabilities. Money for an emergency or a fixed near-term payment belongs in cash or suitable high-quality debt. Long-horizon money can carry equity risk because the date can move and new earnings can refill the plan. Gold is neither a growth company nor a contractual rupee payment; it is a diversifier whose return comes from the metal price, currency and demand.

A policy can be simple: target weights, an allowed band around each weight, which account holds each asset, and a review date. The weights are personal constraints, not market forecasts. A sixty/thirty/ten equity/debt/gold example below explains the mechanics; it is not a recommended mix.

## Rebalancing is a rule, not a prediction

Rebalancing sells some of what became overweight and buys what became underweight. It does not require a view on tomorrow’s market. Two workable rules are calendar rebalancing — review on a fixed annual or half-yearly date — and band rebalancing, where action begins only after an asset leaves its stated range.

:::example A fall restores the target mechanically
Begin with ₹60,00,000 in equity, ₹30,00,000 in debt and ₹10,00,000 in gold. Equity then falls twenty per cent while the other two assets are unchanged. The portfolio is now ₹48,00,000, ₹30,00,000 and ₹10,00,000, or ₹88,00,000 in total.

Restoring sixty/thirty/ten means ₹52,80,000 in equity, ₹26,40,000 in debt and ₹8,80,000 in gold. Buy ₹4,80,000 of equity, sell ₹3,60,000 of debt and sell ₹1,20,000 of gold. The rule buys the fallen asset without claiming to know that the bottom is in.
:::

Use new contributions first. Direct the next SIPs to the underweight asset, which can restore the mix without realising a taxable gain or paying an exit load. When a sale is needed, check lot-level gains and account rules before acting. The [SIP guide](../sip-maths-and-sequencing/) explains why contributions change the sequence.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Bands reduce needless trading, but a band is not an edge. A narrow band trades often and creates tax and friction; a wide one allows risk to drift. The correct width is the one the investor can follow through a sharp move and that keeps the goal’s risk within its written limit.

## What the 2022 lesson does and does not show

{{label:EVIDENCE}} Damodaran’s [US historical-return series](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) records that US stocks and the ten-year Treasury both fell sharply in 2022 while gold was roughly flat. The observation belongs to the **US market**. It demonstrates that stocks and conventional bonds can fall together when inflation and rates rise, and that a third asset can behave differently in one regime.

It does not establish an India equity/debt/gold correlation, an ideal gold weight or a repeatable crisis return. No primary Indian correlation study was fetched for this guide. Indian claims therefore stay {{label:MECHANISM}}: gold may diversify because its drivers differ, but it can fall, spend years going sideways and create tracking and tax friction. The later global allocation guide can place the US episode in its own market context.

## Sovereign Gold Bonds are now a run-off book

The [verified register entry for new SGB issues](../../facts/#fact-inv-sgb-new-issue-status) records the status that cannot be reduced to one short number: the last tranche closed in February 2024, no new tranche has opened since, and RBI now publishes premature-redemption windows for outstanding series. RBI’s [August 2026 calendar](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63416) is direct evidence of that run-off administration.

A [Rajya Sabha reply](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/269/AU1050_MPBCiS.pdf?source=pqars) lists issuance through 2024-25, interest and redemption liabilities, and the Gold Reserve Fund created to manage them. It supports the status of the outstanding book; it does not promise a new issue.

Existing SGBs still have four separate questions:

1. **Metal exposure:** the redemption value follows the scheme’s published gold-price formula.
2. **Interest:** the coupon is taxable as income, independent of the gold-price result.
3. **Exit:** exchange liquidity and the market price matter for a secondary-market sale; RBI’s early-redemption dates apply only to eligible tranches and windows.
4. **Tax:** from the Finance Act 2026 change, capital-gains exemption on redemption is {{fact:inv-sgb-redemption-exemption}}. A secondary-market buyer should not assume the original-holder exemption.

{{label:LORE}} Press accounts give several reasons for stopping new issuance, including the government’s borrowing cost. The primary documents here establish run-off and redemption, not one official causal finding. Do not build a portfolio on a rumour that issuance will restart.

## Gold ETFs after SGB

A gold ETF trades on an exchange and holds gold under a scheme document. It gives daily liquidity and no maturity date, but it does not give the SGB coupon or the original-holder redemption exemption. The market price can differ slightly from NAV, and the return trails physical gold through TER, cash and tracking difference.

SEBI’s [mutual-fund master circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/mar-2026/1774024028162.pdf) requires a gold-ETF scheme document to disclose tracking error and tracking difference, price volatility, liquidity, storage risks and applicable tax. Read those five items, then compare:

- long-horizon tracking difference, not one day’s discount;
- bid–ask spread and traded volume in the units actually used;
- creation-unit liquidity and authorised participants;
- TER, exit path and whether a fund-of-fund adds another cost layer;
- the tax rule for the exact listed ETF or unlisted fund-of-fund form.

The [register’s gold-ETF tax row](../../facts/#fact-inv-gold-etf-tax-rule) records the current rule: listed units cross into long-term treatment after the listed-security holding period and then use the general long-term rate without indexation; a shorter holding is taxed at slab. Gold fund-of-fund units can have a different holding-period test, so the wrapper name matters.

:::example Tax changes the comparison
Assume a ₹2,00,000 gain on listed gold-ETF units after fourteen months. Under the registered long-term rule, the tax is ₹25,000 before cess and ₹26,000 after {{fact:tax-cess-rate}}. If the same gain is short-term and the investor is in the highest displayed slab, {{fact:tax-slab-7}}, tax plus cess can be ₹62,400. These are arithmetic illustrations before surcharge and loss set-off; [Tax for investors](../tax-for-investors/) covers the return-level calculation.
:::

## Turn the mix into operating rules

1. **Write one target for each goal.** Retirement, a home deposit and emergency cash need different mixes and dates.
2. **Name the instruments.** “Debt” might mean a PPF balance, EPF, a gilt fund or a bank deposit; their access and tax differ. [Wrappers](../wrappers-nps-ppf-epf-elss-reits/) maps them.
3. **Choose a rebalance trigger.** One calendar review plus a band check is enough. Record whether new money acts first.
4. **Count locked assets.** EPF, PPF and NPS are part of allocation even when they sit outside the broker statement.
5. **Keep gold’s job explicit.** Diversifier and liquidity reserve, not a return target. Choose ETF, existing SGB or physical form based on access, spread, storage and tax.
6. **Stress the date.** Model an equity fall shortly before the goal and decide which asset funds spending without a forced sale.
7. **Review rules, not headlines.** Change the policy when the goal, income or tolerance changes, not because one asset led the latest year.

- {{label:EVIDENCE}} RBI and Parliament document the outstanding SGB book and redemption calendar; the Income-tax Act states the narrowed exemption; SEBI states the gold-ETF disclosures.
- {{label:EVIDENCE}} The 2022 stock-and-bond observation is US data from Damodaran’s series. It is not presented as Indian correlation evidence.
- {{label:MECHANISM}} Goal-based allocation, contribution-first rebalancing and bands are disciplines that control risk; no source here identifies an optimal mix.
