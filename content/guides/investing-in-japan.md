## Put the recent rally beside the long drought

Japan is the clearest warning against judging a country from one trailing chart. The market experienced a long period of weak nominal growth, deflation and de-rating after the late-1980s bubble, then a powerful recent run as profits, governance and inflation expectations changed.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} The [MSCI Japan factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/b3ee6464-f705-4d65-81a0-d8756607cf9f), dated {{date:2026-09-30}}, reported a gross US-dollar return of 27.04% over the latest year and 25.05% in calendar 2025. It also reported 9.96% a year over ten years but only 3.37% a year since {{date:1994-05-31}}. The start dates differ, dividends are reinvested and the returns are before fund expenses and Indian tax.

The recent period may reflect real changes. It may also contain valuation expansion and a favourable currency path. A country allocation needs a forward thesis about cash flows and price, not a conclusion that the strongest recent line will continue.

## What the Tokyo Stock Exchange is changing

Since March 2023, the Tokyo Stock Exchange has asked companies on its Prime and Standard markets to explain and act on management that is conscious of cost of capital and share price. The exchange’s [fourth-year review](https://www.jpx.co.jp/english/corporate/news/news-releases/1020/20261027-01.html), released on {{date:2026-10-27}}, tracks companies’ disclosures and progress.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} This is an exchange-led governance programme, not a promise of index performance. The mechanism is that a company trading persistently below book value or earning less than its cost of capital is pushed to diagnose why, disclose a plan and improve capital allocation. Possible actions include disposing of cross-shareholdings, raising operating returns, returning surplus cash or investing in projects that clear the cost of capital.

Three checks keep the narrative honest:

1. **Disclosure is not execution.** A plan needs cash-flow and balance-sheet evidence in later filings.
2. **A buyback is not automatically value creation.** It helps only when shares are repurchased below intrinsic value without weakening the business.
3. **An index average hides dispersion.** Exporters, domestic services, banks and manufacturers respond differently to rates and the yen.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Governance reform can improve the use of capital, while a higher valuation can absorb that improvement before an investor arrives. Read return on equity, cash conversion, cross-holdings and payout beside the price paid.

## Valuation and expected return

[AQR’s 2026 capital-market assumptions](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---2026-Capital-Market-Assumptions.pdf?sc_lang=en) estimated a 4.9% local real return for Japanese equities at the end of 2025. The model combines starting valuation, payout, growth and normalisation. It is useful as a planning range and unsuitable as a one-year target.

Japan’s index composition differs from the US. Industrials, consumer businesses, financials and technology suppliers all matter, and the largest global platform companies carry less weight. That can diversify a US-heavy portfolio. It also creates sensitivity to global manufacturing, trade, energy imports and Chinese demand.

The [CAPE and expected-return guide](../cape-expected-returns-and-currency/) explains why a lower starting multiple can raise a modelled return without guaranteeing a faster rerating. Apply the same framework to Japan: starting earnings yield, sustainable growth, payout and a range of ending valuations.

## Yen exposure has two currency steps

A Japan fund may trade in yen, dollars, pounds or euros. The dealing currency changes settlement, not the currency of the companies’ assets and earnings. A dollar-priced Japan ETF generally converts the yen portfolio into a dollar NAV, unless it explicitly hedges the yen.

For an Indian resident, the unhedged path is:

`Japanese equity return × yen movement against the fund currency × fund-currency movement against INR`

The terms multiply. A Japanese exporter may benefit operationally from a weak yen while the Indian holder loses some currency value on conversion. A stronger yen can help the translated return while pressuring an exporter’s profit. Those effects can partly offset or reinforce each other.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Currency hedging removes some yen movement at a cost and introduces hedge-roll tracking. It does not remove company exposure to exchange rates. Check whether the share class is hedged, which currency is hedged and whether the benchmark is hedged before comparing two returns.

## Worked long-horizon arithmetic

:::example The annual rate dominates over decades
Apply the MSCI Japan since-1994 annualised rate of 3.37% to a hypothetical $10,000 for thirty-two years:

`$10,000 × (1.0337)^32 ≈ $28,900`

For scale only, applying the MSCI ACWI factsheet’s 8.89% annualised rate to the same amount and horizon gives:

`$10,000 × (1.0889)^32 ≈ $152,600`

These are mathematical extensions, not two realised investments over identical dates: MSCI Japan’s series starts in 1994, while the [ACWI factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/8d97d244-4685-4200-a24c-3e2942e3adeb) reports its since-inception rate from the end of 1987. Both are gross US-dollar index returns before fees, withholding, Indian tax and the investor’s actual cash flows.
:::

The exercise shows why a long drought matters even after a strong recent year. It does not show that Japan will repeat the lower line or ACWI the higher one.

## Choose the access route before the ticker

An Indian resident can reach Japan through a broad world fund, a dedicated Ireland-domiciled UCITS fund, a US-listed Japan fund or an India-domiciled international scheme when subscriptions are open. Each route changes the legal asset owned.

- A **world fund** gives Japan its market weight and avoids a separate country-rebalance decision.
- An **Ireland-domiciled UCITS fund** is non-US property for US estate-tax purposes, but its own domicile, withholding and Indian reporting still matter.
- A **US-listed Japan fund** is a US-situs security even though it holds Japanese shares. Direct US-situs assets face the non-resident estate threshold of {{fact:glb-us-estate-exemption-nra}}.
- An **India-domiciled scheme** gives rupee dealing and domestic statements but sits inside the industry overseas cap and may pause inflows.

[Interactive Brokers India](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.in/en/accounts/individual-account-steps-in.php) says residents may access foreign stocks, bonds and ETFs but not overseas leverage, futures or options. [Investing abroad from India](../investing-abroad-from-india/) covers LRS, custody, tax and Schedule FA. These are route descriptions, not fund recommendations.

## A Japan allocation checklist

1. Read the index country, size and sector methodology.
2. Separate the long return record from the latest year and keep both dates.
3. Test the governance thesis against company cash flow and capital allocation, not disclosure counts alone.
4. Identify the fund domicile, listing exchange, trading currency and currency-hedge policy.
5. Compare TER, tracking difference, spread, assets and tax reporting.
6. Measure overlap with the global core; Japan is already part of developed-world funds.
7. Set a maximum regional tilt and a calendar rebalance rule before the next headline.

:::evidence What the evidence says
- {{label:EVIDENCE}} MSCI supplies the dated gross return record; JPX documents the governance programme; AQR supplies a disclosed expected-return model.
- {{label:MECHANISM}} Capital allocation, index composition and the two-step currency translation explain possible outcomes. They do not predict a return.
- {{label:LORE}} Governance reform makes every Japanese company cheap, or a dollar listing removes yen risk. Neither statement follows from the cited documents.
