## Start with the evidence, not the label

A factor is a repeatable characteristic associated with differences in returns across many securities. A factor **portfolio** in a paper is not automatically an ETF an investor can own. Academic portfolios may be long one group and short another, rebalance without retail taxes, use tiny companies and ignore the capacity or spread faced by a fund.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} The [Kenneth French Data Library](https://mba.tuck.dartmouth.edu/pages/faculty/ken.french/data_library.html) publishes the market, size, value, profitability, investment and momentum series for the US and developed regions. It is the free primary dataset behind much factor research. The [Fama–French 1993 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(93)90023-5) formalised market, size and value factors; the [2015 paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2014.10.010) added profitability and investment.

The papers explain historical cross-sections. They do not say that a fund with “factor” in its name will outperform after its fee. Before using the evidence, map four gaps:

1. the paper’s definition versus the index rule;
2. the index rule versus the fund portfolio;
3. the published index return versus the fund’s tracking difference;
4. the fund return versus the Indian holder’s tax and currency result.

The [India factor-index guide](../factor-indices-evidence/) applies the same distinction to NSE strategy indices and their back-filled histories.

## What the main tilts require the investor to bear

**Value** owns companies priced cheaply against book value, earnings or cash flow. The reason may be temporary neglect or permanent damage. A value investor earns nothing merely for finding a low multiple; the business must survive and the valuation gap must close.

**Size** gives more weight to smaller companies. They can have less analyst coverage and more room to grow, but also thinner liquidity, weaker financing access and higher failure risk. A total-market fund already owns them at small weights. A size tilt deliberately increases that exposure.

**Profitability and conservative investment** prefer companies earning more on capital and expanding assets less aggressively. The intuition is that a sound business should be worth more than a weak one at the same price. The hard part is valuation: a wonderful company bought at an extreme multiple can still deliver a poor return.

**Momentum** owns recent winners and removes them when leadership changes. It is not in the Fama–French five-factor model, but the French library publishes a momentum series. It turns over more and can reverse abruptly. A momentum fund therefore needs a larger gross premium merely to clear spreads, tax and tracking.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Factor premia can disappear for years because the underlying risk is painful or because investors crowd into the trade. That drought is not a bug that can be diversified away by renaming the same holdings. Value, dividend growth and quality often overlap; size and value may load on distressed companies; profitability and low volatility may crowd into defensive sectors.

## Dividend growth is a screen, not extra return

The [S&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats index](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/dividends-factors/sp-500-dividend-aristocrats/) selects S&P 500 members with at least twenty-five consecutive years of dividend increases and equal-weights them. Its pre-launch history is back-tested, a distinction the index provider discloses.

A long dividend-growth record can signal durable cash generation, board discipline and a mature business. It can also bias the portfolio toward established sectors, exclude firms that reinvest profit well and encourage investors to ignore valuation. A dividend is not a bonus on top of return. On the ex-dividend date the company has less cash, and total return combines the distribution with the price change.

For an Indian resident, the cash distribution creates yearly tax administration. With a valid W-8BEN, US portfolio dividends are normally withheld at {{fact:glb-us-dividend-withholding-dtaa}} under the India–US treaty. India taxes the gross dividend at the investor’s slab and allows foreign tax credit within the treaty limit, subject to the filing process. Price appreciation defers Indian capital-gains tax until sale.

:::example A US dividend received in India
Assume a US ETF pays an Indian resident a $1,000 dividend and USD/INR is ₹90. US withholding at {{fact:glb-us-dividend-withholding-dtaa}} is $250, or ₹22,500, leaving $750 in cash. The gross ₹90,000 still enters the Indian return.

At an assumed thirty-per-cent slab, Indian tax is ₹27,000; the health and education cess of {{fact:tax-cess-rate}} adds ₹1,080, taking the bill to ₹28,080. A full ₹22,500 foreign-tax credit leaves ₹5,580 payable in India. This ignores surcharge and exchange-rate conventions in the return. It shows why dividend yield and after-tax income are different numbers.
:::

## Quality and the Buffett-style mechanism

[AQR’s Quality Minus Junk paper](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Quality-Minus-Junk) defines quality through profitability, growth and safety and tests the signal in the US and across international markets. [Buffett’s Alpha](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Buffetts-Alpha) argues that Berkshire Hathaway’s historical result can be understood through exposure to cheap, safe, high-quality companies, consistent leverage and the ability to survive large drawdowns.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} Those papers are evidence about historical portfolios and one long corporate record. They are not permission to call any high-ROE stock “the next Berkshire”. Buffett-style analysis has three linked questions:

- **Quality:** are profits supported by cash, balance-sheet resilience and durable economics?
- **Price:** what growth and margin path is already embedded in the valuation?
- **Behaviour:** can the owner hold through a large relative and absolute drawdown without forced leverage reduction?

Leverage matters because it magnifies both the quality exposure and the path risk. A retail investor copying visible equity holdings does not copy the financing, insurance float, private businesses, taxes or transaction history that produced Berkshire’s record.

## Turn a factor idea into an investable rule

A practical factor note can fit on one page:

1. **Hypothesis.** State why the factor may persist. “It went up” is not a hypothesis.
2. **Definition.** Record the universe, metric, rebalance frequency, weighting and constraints from the current index methodology.
3. **Live evidence.** Separate back-filled index history from live index history and live fund returns.
4. **Implementation.** Record TER, tracking difference, turnover, bid–ask spread, fund size and tax domicile.
5. **Overlap.** Compare holdings and sectors with the broad-index core and every other tilt.
6. **Pain budget.** Decide the maximum allocation and how long a lag against the core can be tolerated.
7. **Exit rule.** Leave when the methodology changes, tracking becomes persistently poor or the portfolio policy changes, not merely after a weak year.

A core-plus-tilt structure makes the cost of being wrong visible. The broad index holds the market. A smaller tilt expresses the hypothesis without letting one academic result dominate the entire plan.

## Valuation still sits above every factor

A factor score ranks securities against one another; it does not prove the group is cheap in absolute terms. In its [2026 capital-market assumptions](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Alternative-Thinking/AQR-Alternative-Thinking---2026-Capital-Market-Assumptions.pdf?sc_lang=en), AQR placed the starting US CAPE near forty and in the upper tail of its post-1980 history. A quality or dividend portfolio can also start at a demanding multiple.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Combine relative selection with an expected-return range. Earnings yield, growth, payout and valuation change create the return; factor labels describe how the portfolio differs from the market. The [CAPE guide](../cape-expected-returns-and-currency/) develops that decomposition. It cannot time next month, but it prevents “quality” from becoming a reason to pay any price.

:::evidence What the evidence says
- {{label:EVIDENCE}} The French library and linked papers document factor portfolios; S&P documents the dividend-growth rule; AQR documents its quality definitions and the Berkshire analysis.
- {{label:MECHANISM}} Dividend tax drag, factor overlap, valuation and the core-plus-tilt structure explain implementation choices. They do not forecast a premium.
- {{label:LORE}} A high dividend, famous brand or factor label automatically makes a stock safer. None of the cited research establishes that shortcut.
:::
