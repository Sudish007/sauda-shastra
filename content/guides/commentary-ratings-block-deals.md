## Where commentary lives, and the deadlines

The number is in the filing; the story is in the call. Both now run to deadlines. {{label:EVIDENCE}} Schedule III of SEBI's [LODR Regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2026/1784630770711.pdf) requires a listed company to disclose the schedule of its analyst and institutional-investor meets at least two working days in advance and to post the presentation before the meeting begins (clause 15(o)). For post-earnings and quarterly calls, clause 15(oa), inserted by the amendment of {{date:2024-12-13}}, sets three clocks: the audio recording goes on the company's website before the next trading day or within twenty-four hours of the call, whichever is earlier; any video follows within forty-eight hours; the transcript is published on the website and filed with the exchanges at the same time, within five working days.

In practice the order of arrival is: results PDF and press release, then the investor presentation (often within the hour), then the call that evening or the next morning, then the audio, then the transcript days later. A trader who waits for the transcript is reading what the market priced a week ago; a trader who listens live hears the question-and-answer session, which is where guidance gets tested.

Where to find them: the exchange announcement pages ([NSE](https://www.nseindia.com/companies-listing/corporate-filings-announcements), [BSE](https://www.bseindia.com/corporates/ann.html)) list the intimation of the call and the transcript filing under the company's name; the company's investor-relations page carries the audio. [Results season: reading a results filing fast](../results-season-playbook/) covers the filing itself.

## What to extract from a call

A call is management's framing of the quarter, and the questions are analysts' attempts to break that framing. {{label:MECHANISM}} Read it with one sheet and six rows.

| Row | What to write down | Why it moves the stock |
|---|---|---|
| Volume against price | How much of revenue growth was units sold and how much was price or mix | Price-led growth reverses when input costs fall; volume-led growth is the business growing |
| Margin drivers | Raw-material trend, operating leverage, one-off costs, currency | The next quarter's margin is forecast from these, not from this quarter's print |
| Capex and funding | Amount, timing, how it is paid for | Debt-funded capex raises finance costs before it raises revenue |
| Working capital | Receivable days, inventory days, advances | Profit without cash is the earliest warning of trouble |
| Order book or pipeline | Size, execution period, cancellations | Visibility is what analysts pay a multiple for |
| Guidance | Exact words, compared with last quarter's exact words | A softened adjective is a downgrade before any analyst writes one |

{{label:EVIDENCE}} Guidance is voluntary. Neither the results format nor Schedule III contains a line for it, so a company that stops giving an outlook has broken no rule, and a change of outlook is a disclosed material event only where the company's own materiality policy treats it as one. That is why the comparison with the previous quarter's wording has to be done by the reader: nobody is required to flag the change.

## Upgrades, downgrades and target prices

{{label:EVIDENCE}} Anyone who publishes a buy, sell or hold recommendation or a price target for a security in India must hold a certificate of registration under SEBI's [Research Analysts Regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2025/1755176387160.pdf) (Reg 3(1)); the regulations define a research report to include such recommendations and set disclosure duties for every report and public appearance. The [SEBI register](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=14) shows who is registered. A headline that says "Brokerage upgrades X to Buy, target raised" is therefore a registered analyst's opinion, disclosed under those rules.

{{label:LORE}} It is also only an opinion. Neither SEBI nor the exchanges publish the hit rate of analyst targets, and no Indian primary source measures how often an upgrade is followed by a rise. Any claim that ratings "work" or "always lag the price" is a practitioner's story. What is observable is narrower: a rating change from a house with a large institutional client base can move a stock on the day, because clients act on it, and the effect is larger when the change is a surprise than when it follows the price.

Two habits keep ratings in their place. Read the reason, not the rating: an upgrade "on valuation" after a fall says nothing about the business, while a target raised for higher margins is a forecast you can check against the next filing. And never adopt a target price as a trade level: it is one input to one analyst's model, with no stop attached. This site prints none of its own, for the same reason the [About page](../../about/) explains.

## Block deals: the window, the size, the print

A block deal is a large pre-negotiated trade executed in a separate exchange window so that neither side has to work the order through the normal book. {{label:EVIDENCE}} SEBI's [circular of {{date:2025-10-08}}](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2025/1759928647818.pdf) rewrote the framework, and NSE's [circular NSE/CMTR/71394](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/CMTR71394.pdf) put the new terms live on {{date:2025-12-08}} — SEBI's own applicability date was the sixtieth day after the circular, {{date:2025-12-07}}, a Sunday. The framework now has:

- **Two windows.** A morning window, {{fact:mkt-block-window-1}}, whose reference price is the previous day's close, and an afternoon window, {{fact:mkt-block-window-2}}, whose reference price is the volume-weighted average of cash-market trades in the stock during {{fact:mkt-block-window-2-reference}}; the exchanges compute and publish that VWAP in the five minutes before the window opens.
- **A band.** Orders must sit within {{fact:sebi-block-deal-band}} of the reference price, subject to surveillance measures and the stock's own price band: NSE's circular adds that a stock already confined to a narrower daily band keeps the narrower band in the block window too.
- **A minimum.** The minimum order is {{fact:sebi-block-deal-min-size}}; every trade must result in delivery and cannot be squared off or reversed.
- **Publication.** The exchanges disseminate scrip, client names, quantity and price the same day after market hours; the terms also apply to the block window of the optional T+0 settlement cycle.

Before {{date:2025-12-08}} the minimum was ten crore rupees and the band one per cent either side, under the framework of October 2017 that SEBI's [master circular for stock exchanges](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/dec-2024/RE_Chapter%201%20-%20Trading%20-%20NEW_p.pdf) of December 2024 still records. Orders smaller than the new minimum go through the normal market like anyone else's.

:::example Worked example
At ₹500 a share, the {{fact:sebi-block-deal-min-size}} minimum is 5,00,000 shares. If the afternoon reference price is ₹500, the {{fact:sebi-block-deal-band}} band allows trades between ₹485 and ₹515, and a fund selling a large stake at ₹490 inside the window prints at a visible discount to the market without touching the normal order book. The trade appears on the exchange's list that evening with both parties named; the stock's normal-market price during the window is a separate matter.
:::

## Bulk deals: the same evening's list

{{label:EVIDENCE}} A bulk deal is not a window; it is a disclosure threshold. Under SEBI's rule of January 2004, restated in the master circular, all of a single client's trades in one scrip in a day that together exceed {{fact:sebi-bulk-deal-threshold}} of the company's listed equity shares, executed in the normal market in one or more trades, must be disclosed by the broker immediately on execution — scrip, client name, quantity and price — and the exchange publishes the list the same day after market hours. The daily lists sit on one [NSE page](https://www.nseindia.com/report-detail/display-bulk-and-block-deals) with the block deals.

A company with twenty crore listed shares therefore shows a bulk-deal print when one client's purchases or sales in a day pass 10,00,000 shares, whatever the price. Because the threshold is a share of the company, bulk prints are common in small companies and rare in large ones, where the same money moves a far smaller fraction of the float.

| Feature | Block deal | Bulk deal |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Separate exchange window | Normal market |
| Trigger | Order of at least the minimum size | A client's day total above the share threshold |
| Price | Within the band of the reference price | Any traded price |
| Counterparty | Negotiated in advance | Whoever was on the book |
| Published | Same day after market hours | Same day after market hours |

## Reading a print honestly

{{label:MECHANISM}} A block or bulk print tells you who moved size and at what price. It does not tell you why. The seller may be a fund rebalancing, a promoter selling to meet a pledge call, a private-equity holder exiting on schedule, or two affiliates of one group crossing shares for a restructuring; the buyer may be an index fund that had no view at all. The names on the list let you check each possibility against the company's filings — a promoter sale shows up in the shareholding pattern and the takeover-code disclosures described in [Governance red flags](../governance-red-flags/) — but the print alone is a fact without a motive.

{{label:LORE}} Whether a stock that prints a large block at a discount tends to fall further, or recover, over the following days has no Indian study behind it that this site could find on {{date:2026-11-02}}. The practitioners' versions ("blocks at a discount are distribution", "institutional buying blocks mark bottoms") contradict each other, which is reason enough to treat both as lore. What a trader can do is mechanical: note the print in the journal, check whether the next day's open gaps away from the block price, and treat a stock with a large seller still at work as one that needs a wider stop, or no trade.
