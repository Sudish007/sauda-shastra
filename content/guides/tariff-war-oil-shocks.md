## Why case studies, not forecasts

A tariff announcement or a missile strike is the kind of event a trader cannot schedule and cannot predict. What can be learned is how such shocks have reached Indian prices, through which channel, with what lag, and which parts of the headline turned out to matter. The two episodes below are told from primary documents — executive orders, a court opinion, the Federal Register, the US Energy Information Administration and the RBI — so that the dates and rates are facts and the trading lessons are clearly labelled as mechanism or lore. Nothing here is a view on any security.

## The US tariffs on India, by date and legal route

Every US tariff rests on a statute, and the statute decided how long each one lasted. Three routes appear in the Indian story: the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), which the President used for the 2025 "reciprocal" rates and the Russia-related duty; Section 122 of the Trade Act 1974, a short-term surcharge power; and Section 301 of the same Act, which targets specific trade practices and takes an investigation to invoke.

The sources are the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/11/2025-15267/addressing-threats-to-the-united-states-by-the-government-of-the-russian-federation) texts of each order and proclamation, the [joint statement](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2224783) as published by PIB, the [Supreme Court's opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf) and the [Congressional Research Service's summary](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/LSB/PDF/LSB11398/LSB11398.1.pdf) of it. Read as a sequence, the framework rate of February 2026 was overtaken within a fortnight: once the court removed the IEEPA basis, Indian goods paid the Section 122 surcharge from {{date:2026-02-24}} and the Section 301 duty from {{date:2026-07-24}}, plus any product-specific duties under other statutes.

:::warning What this site does not print
Press reports in late 2025 added India's reciprocal rate to the Russia-related duty and ran a single combined headline figure. The reciprocal component exists only in the image annex of Executive Order 14326, which cannot be read as text in any official copy fetched for this site. Until a text source such as the Harmonized Tariff Schedule's chapter 99 notes is checked, the combined figure is not stated here as a fact — only the rates that appear in text are, and each of them is in the [verified facts register](../../facts/).
:::

:::example
What the routes meant for one shipment. On a $10,000 consignment of Indian goods, the Russia-related duty alone added $2,500 at the {{fact:mac-us-tariff-india-eo14329}} rate (10,000 × the rate), before the reciprocal rate that cannot be quoted here. Under the February 2026 framework the reciprocal duty would have been $1,800 at the {{fact:mac-us-tariff-india-framework}} rate. After the court ruling the same consignment paid $1,000 under the Section 122 surcharge at {{fact:mac-us-tariff-s122-surcharge}}, and from July 2026 $1,000 under Section 301 at {{fact:mac-us-tariff-india-s301}}. Four regimes in eleven months, each with a known start date — and the start dates, not the rhetoric around them, were the tradable facts.
:::

### What the market could and could not price

{{label:MECHANISM}} A tariff is a cash-flow event for the companies that export the taxed goods and a sentiment event for everyone else. The first group's revenue changes by a knowable amount from a knowable date; the second group's price changes because risk appetite does. The distinction decides the horizon. A cash-flow hit persists as long as the duty does and shows up in results a quarter later; a sentiment hit fades as soon as the next headline arrives. The currency channel sits between the two: a worse export outlook weakens the rupee at the margin, which, as [FII and DII flows, the rupee and the RBI](../flows-rupee-and-rbi/) explains, helps the dollar-earning sectors the tariff was not aimed at.

The legal route added a second layer. Duties imposed under IEEPA carried a court risk from the day they were signed; the Section 122 surcharge carried a 150-day clock written into the statute; the Section 301 notice rests on a completed investigation and names no end date. A trader who read the statute knew which duties had an end date and which did not. That is dull work, and it is the work.

{{label:LORE}} How many days a tariff headline takes to fade from Nifty, or which export sectors "always" fall most — there is no primary Indian study of either, and this site does not supply a number. The dates are facts; the fade is folklore until measured.

## Two oil shocks

### June 2025: a spike and a round trip

{{label:EVIDENCE}} The US Energy Information Administration's [account](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=65884) is the clean record. After Israel's strikes on Iran on {{date:2025-06-13}}, Brent rose from $69 a barrel to $79 in the week to {{date:2025-06-19}} on the risk to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz; the United States struck on {{date:2025-06-21}}, Iran responded on {{date:2025-06-23}}, a ceasefire followed and prices fell back. The same note records that inflation-adjusted Brent had fallen from about $75 in early April 2025 to $64 in June, its lowest since December 2020, on fears that the April tariffs would slow global growth.

The two shocks of 2025 therefore pulled in opposite directions for India. Tariffs hurt exporters and, through growth fears, lowered the price of the country's largest import; the Gulf conflict then raised that price for a week before the ceasefire took it back. A trader who reacted only to the headline would have positioned for a sustained rise that lasted a week; one who followed the channel in [Crude, US yields, the dollar and the sector map](../crude-yields-dollar-sector-map/) would have asked first whether a one-week spike was likely to reach pump prices and marketing margins at all.

### September 2026: a shock that stayed

The second episode did not reverse within the week. The RBI's [Governor's statement of {{date:2026-10-07}}](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63744) recorded that the West Asia conflict re-escalated in September 2026, hardening crude and raising market volatility, and that government bond yields had risen from mid-August into September on geopolitics, global yields and crude. The Indian crude basket averaged $82.0 a barrel in July 2026, $90.2 in August and $116.1 in September — a rise of about $34 a barrel in two months, with Brent's September average more than a fifth above July's.

:::example
Scale the September move with the import arithmetic from the sector-map guide: about 4.9 million barrels a day imported, so $34 a barrel is roughly $167 million a day, or about ₹1,585 crore a day at ₹94.94 per dollar — on the order of ₹47,000 crore over a thirty-day month that India did not have to spend in July. The estimate ignores hedging, product trade and the lag between a cargo's pricing and its arrival, but the order of magnitude is right, and it is why a sustained oil shock reaches the rupee, the bond market and the RBI's inflation projection, not only the oil stocks.
:::

The RBI's own response followed on {{date:2026-10-07}}: a quarter-point increase in the repo rate to {{fact:mac-repo-rate}} and a stance of calibrated tightening, documented in [Policy days, India VIX and sizing](../policy-days-vix-and-sizing/). An oil shock that persists becomes a rates story within weeks; the June 2025 shock, which reversed, never did.

## Headline to sector map to fade or follow

The two episodes suggest a sequence of questions. None of them says which way to trade; together they say whether a trade is a reaction to information or a reaction to adrenaline.

1. **Does the headline change cash flows, and whose?** A duty on Indian exports changes exporters' revenue from a date; a strike on an oil facility changes the price of an import. Name the companies whose income statement moves. If you cannot, the event is sentiment.
2. **Which channel?** Run the [sector map](../crude-yields-dollar-sector-map/): rupee, crude, rates, China. An oil shock that pump prices do not follow is a marketing-margin story, not a consumer story; a tariff that weakens the rupee is a mild positive for IT services while it is a negative for exporters of goods.
3. **Does the shock have a date on it?** A statute with a 150-day clock, a court hearing with a known date, a ceasefire deadline: scheduled resolutions behave like the policy days above, with implied volatility bid up into them and crushed after. Open-ended shocks do not crush on a date.
4. **How much is already priced?** Read the straddle-implied move, as [Implied volatility, the expected move and IV crush](../iv-expected-move-and-crush/) describes, before deciding that a reaction is "overdone". A two per cent gap on a shock that the options were pricing at three is not an overreaction.
5. **Size for the regime you are in, not the one you remember.** The India VIX arithmetic in [Policy days, India VIX and sizing](../policy-days-vix-and-sizing/) gives the one-day σ; an open-ended geopolitical shock is the case for the smaller of the two sizes there, and for defined-risk structures such as the hedged event-day position in the [playbook](../../playbooks/event-day-iv-crush-hedged/) rather than naked exposure held overnight.

{{label:MECHANISM}} Fading or following is then a statement about horizon, not about courage. A sentiment shock is faded because it has no cash-flow anchor; a cash-flow shock is followed because the first day's move rarely prices a change that lasts quarters. Deciding which kind you are looking at is the whole skill, and the two episodes above show that the same headline — "conflict in West Asia" — was one kind in June 2025 and the other in September 2026.

:::warning The trap in shock trading
The expensive mistakes in shock trading are behavioural: chasing the move an hour after the headline because the screen is red, averaging into a falling exporter because "it will come back", and widening a stop because the news "changed the thesis". [Psychology, journaling and prop firms](../psychology-journaling-prop-firms/) covers the controls. Shock days are the days the daily loss limit exists for.
:::
