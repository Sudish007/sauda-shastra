## Why crude matters more here than in New York

Crude up ten dollars is a sector story in New York and a macro story in Mumbai, because India buys most of its oil abroad. India's crude-oil import dependence, measured on the basis of petroleum-product consumption, has stayed at about {{fact:mac-crude-import-dependence}} for three financial years running, according to the [petroleum ministry's answer in the Rajya Sabha](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/270/AU4124_gs8kNF.pdf?source=pqars) of {{date:2026-03-30}}. Domestic production fell by nearly a quarter over the decade, from 37.461 million tonnes in 2014-15 to 28.704 million tonnes in 2024-25, as fields such as Mumbai High aged. The gap is bought abroad in dollars.

The scale is visible in the trade data. The RBI's [Governor's statement of {{date:2026-10-07}}](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63744) put the merchandise trade deficit for July–August 2026 at $58.7 billion, against $55.1 billion a year earlier, with imports of petroleum, crude and products at $35.0 billion in those two months, up 21.3 per cent on a year earlier. The price India pays is tracked by the Petroleum Planning and Analysis Cell as the Indian crude basket, a weighted average of the sour and sweet grades the country actually buys: it averaged $82.0 a barrel in July 2026, $90.2 in August and $116.1 in September, a rise of 41.6 per cent in two months as the West Asia conflict re-escalated (PPAC figures cited in the same RBI statement).

### A back-of-envelope import bill

:::example
PPAC's [import report](https://ppac.gov.in/import-export) shows 123,290 thousand tonnes of crude imported in April–September 2026, the first half of the financial year. Over 183 days that is about 674 thousand tonnes a day; at roughly 7.33 barrels to a tonne, about 4.9 million barrels a day. Every $10 a barrel on that volume is about $49 million a day, which at ₹94.94 per dollar (the FBIL reference rate of {{date:2026-10-23}}) is about ₹465 crore a day, or ₹14,000 crore in a thirty-day month.

So the September 2026 basket, $34 a barrel above July's, cost India on the order of ₹1,585 crore a day more than July's did. The arithmetic is an estimate — it ignores product imports, hedging and the lag between cargo pricing and delivery — but it is the right order of magnitude, and it is why crude is a rupee story and a current-account story before it is a stock story.
:::

At the pump the pass-through is political. PPAC's [daily price page](https://ppac.gov.in/) showed Delhi petrol at ₹102.12 a litre and diesel at ₹95.20 on {{date:2026-10-30}}; when retail prices lag crude, the difference sits on the oil marketing companies' books. That is the first row of the sector map below.

## US yields and the dollar

The second channel is the price of money in the world's reserve currency. On {{date:2026-10-30}} the [US Treasury's par yield curve](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value_month=202610) read 4.56 per cent at two years, 4.71 at five, 4.94 at ten and 5.31 at thirty; on {{date:2026-10-01}} the ten-year had printed 5.24 per cent. The RBI's [October 2026 resolution](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63742) noted that global bond yields were at record highs amid fiscal concerns, after the Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by a quarter point in September 2026. The Fed then held its target range at {{fact:mac-fed-funds-range}} on {{date:2026-10-28}} by a ten-to-two vote, the two dissenters preferring another quarter-point increase ([FOMC statement](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)).

{{label:MECHANISM}} Why does an Indian trader care about a US bond? Because a dollar deposit paying close to five per cent competes with every emerging-market asset for the same foreign capital. When US yields rise faster than Indian yields, the return for carrying rupee risk shrinks, foreign portfolio money leaves at the margin, and the rupee weakens — the flow channel described in [FII and DII flows, the rupee and the RBI](../flows-rupee-and-rbi/). The channel runs through flows and the currency, with a lag of days to weeks, not through Nifty's earnings.

### What DXY is and is not

The US Dollar Index, quoted as DXY, is a geometric average of the dollar against six currencies with weights fixed since the euro's launch in 1999, the euro block alone carrying 57.6 per cent; ICE Data Indices computes it in real time ([ICE](https://www.ice.com/forex/usdx)). The rupee is not in it. A rising DXY usually goes with a weaker rupee because both reflect dollar strength, but the two can and do diverge when the move is about the euro or the yen rather than about emerging markets. Watch USD/INR itself for the Indian channel; use DXY only as a reading of the dollar's general direction.

## The sector map

Each row is a mechanism with a direction and a lag. None is a trading rule on its own: the final column says what breaks it, and the price of the sector relative to Nifty is the check.

| Sector | Driver | What usually follows, and the lag | What breaks the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| IT services | USD/INR | {{label:MECHANISM}} Exporters bill mostly in dollars and pay costs in rupees, so a weaker rupee lifts reported rupee revenue and margins; the stocks react the same day, the accounts a quarter later | Client spending in the US matters more than the currency; hedging programmes and euro and sterling moves dilute the effect |
| Oil marketing companies | Crude (Indian basket) | {{label:MECHANISM}} When retail prices lag crude, marketing margins absorb the gap, which PPAC tracks as under-recoveries; inventory reprices over days to weeks | A retail price change or government compensation; refining margins that move the other way |
| Banks | RBI repo rate and bond yields | {{label:MECHANISM}} Lending rates reprice before deposit rates after a policy move, so margins move over quarters; a jump in yields marks down the bond book at once | Credit growth and asset quality dominate; a rate rise in a strong economy is not bad for banks |
| Metals | China's demand for steel | {{label:MECHANISM}} China's demand sets global steel and base-metal prices, which Indian producers take; weeks of lag | Import duties and safeguards, and domestic demand, decouple Indian prices from the global curve |

### IT services and the rupee

Indian IT exporters earn in dollars, euros and sterling and pay salaries in rupees. [Infosys's integrated annual report](https://www.infosys.com/investors/reports-filings/annual-report/annual-reports/ar-2025-26.html), like its peers', reports results in both currencies and discusses the currency effect; the direction is the same for the whole sector. The trap is to stop at the rupee. A weaker rupee that comes with a US slowdown — the usual pairing, since both follow a stronger dollar — can be worse for the sector than a stable rupee with healthy client budgets.

### Oil marketers and crude

The state-owned marketers sell petrol and diesel at prices that do not move daily with the basket. PPAC publishes the resulting [under-recoveries on sensitive products](https://ppac.gov.in/prices/under-recoveries-to-oil-companies-on-sale-of-sensitive-petroleum-products-rs-crore) alongside the daily retail prices, so the squeeze is measurable rather than assumed. Crude up and pump prices unchanged is bad for the marketers and good for everyone who buys fuel; crude down with pump prices unchanged is the reverse. The refining business inside the same companies can move the other way, which is why the stocks do not track crude tick for tick.

### Banks and rates

The RBI's own transmission numbers show the lag. In the [October 2026 statement](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=63744) the weighted average lending rate on fresh rupee loans rose by eight basis points over July–August 2026 while the rate on fresh deposits fell by 28 basis points: the two sides of a bank's balance sheet reprice at different speeds, and the gap between them is the margin. A policy move at {{fact:mac-rbi-mpc-time-ist}} on an RBI day therefore reaches bank earnings over quarters, while the bond book reprices the same afternoon. [Policy days, India VIX and sizing](../policy-days-vix-and-sizing/) covers the day itself.

### Metals and China

Steel is the clearest case. The World Steel Association's [October 2026 outlook](https://worldsteel.org/media/press-releases/2026/worldsteel-short-range-outlook-october-2026/) saw global demand flat at 1,722 million tonnes in 2026 and up 1.7 per cent to 1,752 million tonnes in 2027, with the downgrade almost entirely due to China, while demand outside China grew 1.5 per cent in 2026 and was expected to grow 4.0 per cent in 2027. Indian metal producers sell into that global price whatever their domestic order books say, which is why a Chinese stimulus headline moves Indian metal stocks in the Hong Kong morning session, hours before NSE opens — one reason the [overnight chain](../overnight-chain-to-india-open/) includes Hong Kong.

## Using the map without over-trusting it

The map tells you where to look, not what to do. Three checks turn a story into a tradable observation.

1. **Is the sector actually behaving as the map says?** Compute its relative strength against Nifty over the last few sessions, as [Multi-timeframe analysis, relative strength and sector rotation](../multi-timeframe-relative-strength/) describes. If crude is up ten per cent and the oil marketers are outperforming, the market knows something the map does not — a price cut already priced in, a compensation package — and the map loses.
2. **Which way is the lag running?** A currency move hits IT stocks today and IT earnings next quarter; a rate move hits bank bond books today and bank margins over a year. Trading the long-lag channel on an intraday horizon is a category error.
3. **What is the surprise, not the level?** The yields and prices above were public on their dates; what moves a sector on a given day is the change against what was expected. A $116 basket that everyone has read about is in the price; the next $10 is not.

Pairs built on these rows — a sector against the index, one sector against another — are the subject of the [relative-strength pairs playbook](../../playbooks/relative-strength-pairs/). Everything there and here is a mechanism to test, not a forecast, and none of the figures above is a recommendation about any security.
