## Why the broad index is the default

A broad index starts from a modest claim: own the market instead of trying to identify tomorrow’s winners. A market-cap-weighted fund increases the weight of companies whose market value grows and removes companies when the index rule says they no longer qualify. It does not inspect management quality or declare a fair price. That simplicity keeps turnover, research cost and manager risk low.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} The [SPIVA U.S. Year-End 2025 scorecard](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us-year-end-2025/) found that {{fact:glb-spiva-us-largecap-20y}} of active US large-cap funds underperformed the S&P 500 over its twenty-year window. This is a US fund result, before survivorship adjustment changes anyone’s personal tax bill. It does not prove that every index fund beats every active fund. It sets a demanding base rate: an active choice needs evidence that survives its higher fee, turnover and manager changes.

The default is useful because it is auditable. The investor can compare the fund with its published benchmark, inspect tracking difference and know what exposure was purchased. “Broad” does not mean safe. A cap-weighted index can be expensive, concentrated in a sector and fall sharply with the market.

## S&P 500 versus the total US market

The S&P 500 holds large US companies selected under an index committee’s eligibility rules. A total-market index adds mid-sized, small and micro-cap companies. The two portfolios still overlap heavily because the largest companies dominate both by value.

The practical difference is exposure, not a permanent return advantage:

| Choice | What it owns | Main omission | Useful question |
|---|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 | Large, established US companies | Smaller listed companies | Is a liquid large-cap core enough? |
| Total US market | Nearly the whole investable US equity market | Non-US companies | Is one broader US holding simpler? |
| Global market | US plus developed and emerging markets | Home-country customisation | Is market-cap weight the neutral starting point? |

The [MSCI ACWI factsheet](https://www.msci.com/documents/10199/8d97d244-4685-4200-a24c-3e2942e3adeb) put the United States at {{fact:glb-acwi-us-weight}} of the global index on {{date:2026-09-30}}. A US fund is therefore a large part of a global portfolio, but it is not the whole world. An Indian investor also already has salary, property, taxes and perhaps most financial assets tied to India. That human-capital exposure matters when deciding how much foreign equity is useful.

## Reinterpreting the three-fund structure from India

The Bogleheads structure is a total US stock fund, a total international stock fund and a broad bond fund. The [Bogle Center](https://boglecenter.net/) is the accessible starting point for that index-first approach; the community wiki was blocked during the source check, so this guide does not rely on an unfetched page.

For a US household, “domestic” means US shares. For an Indian household, the labels need translating:

1. **Indian core:** the domestic equity holding, usually a broad Indian index or a documented direct-equity process.
2. **Foreign equity:** US and developed or emerging markets. A total-US fund can be the main foreign leg, while an ex-US fund prevents the foreign allocation from becoming a single-country bet.
3. **Goal-matched debt:** rupee liabilities usually call for rupee debt. A US bond fund adds dollar duration and currency movement; it is not a direct replacement for PPF, EPF, a rupee deposit or an Indian bond allocation.

{{label:MECHANISM}} The structure is a checklist, not a prescribed split. It forces three decisions into the open: domestic versus foreign equity, equity versus debt, and which currency should fund the goal. The [India index guide](../index-funds-india/) handles the home core. The [investing-abroad guide](../investing-abroad-from-india/) handles remittance, tax and account routes.

## What low cost looks like

The following Vanguard funds are examples of categories, not product recommendations: [VTI](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vti/vanguard-total-stock-market-etf) for total US shares, [VXUS](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vxus/vanguard-total-international-stock-etf) for shares outside the US and [BND](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/bnd/vanguard-total-bond-market-etf) for the broad US investment-grade bond market.

Put $10,000 into a hypothetical 60/30/10 VTI/VXUS/BND mix. The provider expenses are {{fact:glb-vti-expense-ratio}}, {{fact:glb-vxus-expense-ratio}} and {{fact:glb-bnd-expense-ratio}}. The weighted expense is 0.036%, or about $3.60 in the first year on an unchanged balance.

The peer average shown beside VOO on Vanguard’s provider page was {{fact:glb-active-us-largecap-peer-expense}}. Applied only as a fee illustration to the same $10,000, that is about $40.50. The $36.90 gap compounds every year; it is not a forecast of either portfolio’s return.

Fund expense is only one layer for an Indian resident. Add the bid–ask spread, broker fee, foreign-exchange spread, transfer charge, withholding and Indian tax. TCS can create a cash-flow cost even when it becomes a tax credit later.

:::example A GIFT City order
Suppose the $10,000 foreign allocation is sent in five $2,000 orders. At the provider’s published brokerage of {{fact:glb-nseixga-brokerage}}, each order costs $5, so five orders cost $25 before the stated tax on brokerage. One $2,000 order has $0.90 of that tax under the provider’s schedule. The point is not that five orders are right; it is that a minimum charge makes order size part of implementation.
:::

## Build an operating rule

A broad-index plan still needs instructions for difficult markets:

- Set the foreign-equity share from goals, currency exposure and tolerance for tracking India differently, not from the latest US return.
- Choose S&P 500 or total market once; do not switch because small companies led one year.
- Decide whether ex-US shares belong inside the foreign allocation. [Vanguard’s diversification research](https://www.vanguardmexico.com/content/dam/intl/americas/documents/mexico/en/global-equity-investing-diversification-sizing.pdf) found the volatility-minimising international share varied across the markets it studied, so there is no universal home-bias number.
- Match debt to the spending currency and horizon. A dollar bond fund can fall when yields rise and can gain or lose in rupees through USD/INR.
- Rebalance on a calendar or band rule. New money can restore weights before a taxable sale.
- Review tracking, custody, account statements and tax records annually. Review the allocation only when the goal changes.

:::evidence What the evidence says
- {{label:EVIDENCE}} SPIVA supplies the dated US active-versus-index base rate; MSCI supplies the dated world weight; Vanguard supplies the fund expenses and diversification study.
- {{label:MECHANISM}} Broad ownership, low turnover and rebalancing explain the three-fund structure. They do not establish a best split for an Indian household.
- {{label:LORE}} “The S&P 500 is always enough” and “more funds always mean more diversification” are slogans, not conclusions from these sources.
:::
