## The index is the hurdle

Buying a company directly is a decision to differ from a low-cost index fund. The hurdle is therefore not whether the company is familiar or profitable. It is whether the investor can explain why this position deserves to replace part of the diversified alternative after research time, tax, trading cost and concentration risk.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} The [Nifty 50 factsheet](https://www.niftyindices.com/Factsheet/ind_nifty50.pdf) dated {{date:2026-10-30}} put Financial Services at {{fact:inv-nifty50-financials-weight}} of the index. A ten- or fifteen-company portfolio built from familiar large companies can quietly recreate that bank-heavy exposure while losing the index’s rebalancing rule. To differ meaningfully, it must concentrate; concentration is also how it can underperform badly.

A useful policy is a core index allocation plus a smaller direct-equity sleeve. Every direct holding then needs a written thesis, a valuation range and a reason it is not already better owned through the core. [Index funds in India](../index-funds-india/) covers the alternative.

## Screen one: returns on the capital inside the business

{{label:MECHANISM}} Return on capital employed asks what operating profit the company produces from equity plus interest-bearing debt used in operations. A simple version is EBIT divided by capital employed. One year is noisy; the direction across a full cycle matters.

Read it with three companions:

- **Incremental return on capital:** the change in operating profit divided by the capital added. A company can report a high historic ROCE while every new project earns less.
- **Free cash flow:** cash from operations minus capital expenditure needed to maintain and grow the business. Accounting profit cannot repay debt; cash can.
- **Cash conversion:** cash from operations against EBITDA or operating profit over several years. Receivables rising much faster than revenue can turn reported growth into financing supplied by shareholders.

The [results-season guide](../results-season-playbook/) shows how to rebuild EBITDA from the prescribed Ind AS lines. For investing, repeat that exercise across years, not one quarter. A low or falling return on capital with rising debt is the classic shape of a value trap: the P/E looks low because the business needs more money to stand still.

Different industries need different readings. Banks do not use an industrial-company ROCE; they need asset quality, margins and capital adequacy. Utilities and infrastructure naturally carry more debt. The screen compares a company with its own economics and peers, not with one universal cut-off.

## Screen two: ownership, pledge and allocation

{{label:EVIDENCE}} Promoter ownership is disclosed in the quarterly shareholding pattern under the [LODR Regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2026/1784630770711.pdf). Promoter encumbrances are disclosed under Regulation 31 of the [takeover regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1785911192198.pdf), and NSE publishes the filings on its [pledged-data page](https://www.nseindia.com/companies-listing/corporate-filings-pledged-data). Read the change, not a folklore danger line.

A rising pledged share can create a feedback loop: price falls, collateral value falls, the lender asks for more collateral, and an invocation creates forced selling. The [governance guide](../governance-red-flags/) sets out the disclosure timing and related-party thresholds. Here it is one part of the ownership test:

1. Did promoter ownership rise through purchases, fall through sales, or change because new shares diluted everyone?
2. Is any holding pledged or otherwise encumbered, and is that share rising?
3. Are minority shareholders treated the same in dividends, buybacks, mergers and related-party deals?
4. Does the annual report reconcile the promoter group across subsidiaries and trusts?

{{label:MECHANISM}} Capital allocation is the next screen. Retained earnings should go to projects that can earn more than the cost of capital. If they cannot, dividends or buybacks may be better. Serial acquisitions, repeated equity issuance, loans to group companies and a growing pile of unrelated businesses require evidence that management can allocate outside the original business. A high ROCE followed by poor acquisitions is a company-level return being diluted by a management-level decision.

## Valuation bands, dated correctly

Valuation is a price paid for a stream of uncertain cash flows. A band is more honest than a single “fair value” because margins, growth and discount rates move.

{{label:EVIDENCE}} The Nifty 50 factsheet’s Fundamentals row on {{date:2026-10-30}} reads:

| Measure | Dated value | What it means |
|---|---:|---|
| Price to earnings | {{fact:inv-nifty50-pe}} | Price divided by trailing index earnings |
| Price to book | {{fact:inv-nifty50-pb}} | Price divided by index book value |
| Dividend yield | {{fact:inv-nifty50-dy}} | Cash dividend divided by price |
| Price return, year to date | {{fact:inv-nifty50-ytd-price}} | Market context for the valuation snapshot |

Those are the corrected column readings from the factsheet. P/B is {{fact:inv-nifty50-pb}}, not the dividend yield, and the yield is {{fact:inv-nifty50-dy}}, not P/B.

:::example Earnings yield as a comparison, not a signal
The inverse of the index P/E, one divided by {{fact:inv-nifty50-pe}}, is about 5.18 per cent. Compare that with the current PPF rate of {{fact:inv-ppf-rate}} only as a starting frame: index earnings can grow or shrink and the price can fall; PPF interest follows a government-notified rate and does not carry equity volatility. The gap is not a buy or sell rule.
:::

Build company bands from its own history and economics. A cyclical business at peak earnings often looks cheapest just before earnings fall. A growing asset-light business can deserve a higher multiple, but only if cash conversion and incremental returns support the growth. Always state whether P/E is trailing or forward and whether exceptional income is removed.

## Market-cap-to-GDP needs two dates

{{label:EVIDENCE}} BSE’s [all-India market-cap table](https://www.bseindia.com/markets/equity/EQReports/allindiamktcap.aspx) put listed value at {{fact:inv-bse-market-cap}} on {{date:2026-10-30}}. MoSPI’s [provisional estimate](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2269286) put nominal GDP for FY 2025-26 at {{fact:inv-nominal-gdp-fy26}}. Dividing the first by the second gives the registered market-cap-to-GDP ratio of about {{fact:inv-mcap-to-gdp}}.

:::example One ratio, two clocks
{{fact:inv-bse-market-cap}} ÷ {{fact:inv-nominal-gdp-fy26}} = roughly {{fact:inv-mcap-to-gdp}}. The numerator is a one-day market price; the denominator is a trailing financial-year flow. A rising market can move the ratio tomorrow while the GDP estimate remains unchanged for months. That convention must travel with the number.
:::

{{label:MECHANISM}} The ratio says how richly the listed market is valued relative to the economy under that convention. It does not say when prices reverse. The listed universe changes, profits can take a different share of GDP, multinational revenue sits outside India, and a large unlisted economy sits in GDP but not market capitalisation. Treat it as a slow context gauge beside earnings yields, rates and profit margins.

## A one-page direct-equity record

Before buying, write one page that can later prove the thesis wrong:

- **Business:** how it makes money, the key volume and price drivers, and the main regulatory risk.
- **Economics:** multi-year ROCE, incremental ROCE, operating margin, cash conversion, debt and interest cover, all from filed accounts.
- **Ownership:** promoter holding, pledge trend, dilution and related-party transactions, linked to exchange filings.
- **Allocation:** where retained cash went and what return those uses produced.
- **Valuation:** base, optimistic and stressed earnings or cash flow, each with a multiple range and explicit assumptions.
- **Portfolio role:** position size, sector exposure after purchase, and which index holding it displaces.
- **Break condition:** a business fact that closes the thesis — lost licence, failed cash conversion, debt breach or allocation outside the mandate — not a live price target.

Review the record after each annual report and material filing. Price alone does not invalidate a business thesis; a business fact does. Conversely, a rising price does not repair weak cash flow or governance.
