## What this site is

Sauda Shastra (सौदा शास्त्र, “the science of the trade”) is a free, independent knowledge site on Indian and global markets for intraday traders, options traders and long-term investors, with a researched library of courses and books. Its method is simple to state. Every statement that matters rests on a primary document (SEBI, NSE, BSE, RBI, the Income-tax Act or a broker’s own page), logged with its address and the date it was read; secondary sources only point the way to primaries. Every number that can change (a rate, a lot size, an expiry day, a threshold, an API price) lives in one [verified facts register](../facts/) and is rendered from there with the date it was last checked, so the same figure never appears in two versions. Rule changes are listed by date on [What changed](../changelog/), and the full [bibliography](../sources/) is one page. Where a claim rests on a study, the study is linked; where it rests on practitioners’ experience alone, the page says so.

## Disclaimer

Educational content, not investment advice. The author is not a SEBI-registered research analyst or investment adviser. Derivatives trading carries risk of loss.

Nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security, derivative or fund. No page names a live instrument with a target or a stop. Worked examples use historical or hypothetical figures and are labelled as such. The site makes no claim about returns, past or future, for any method it describes.

## What SEBI’s rules mean for this site

Two sets of SEBI regulations draw the line between education and advice, and two circulars spell out how SEBI reads that line for people who publish about markets. The documents below were read on {{date:2026-11-01}}; the versions linked are the consolidated texts current on that date.

- **Research analysts.** The [SEBI (Research Analysts) Regulations, 2014](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1785912002778.pdf), as amended to {{date:2025-11-25}}, define research services to include buy, sell or hold recommendations, price or stop-loss targets, model portfolios and trading calls. Anyone who offers those for a fee must be registered as a research analyst, must keep records, and may not deal in a security they cover for a window around publication. A part-time registration exists, capped at seventy-five clients. This site publishes none of those services.
- **Investment advisers.** The [SEBI (Investment Advisers) Regulations, 2013](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2026/1785912187595.pdf), as amended to {{date:2025-11-25}}, cover personalised advice given for a fee, with the adviser acting as a fiduciary. The regulations say that information given through a medium open to the public, such as a website, is not investment advice, and that trading calls belong under the research-analyst rules rather than these. Nothing here is personal to any reader.
- **Association with unregistered persons.** [SEBI circular CIR/2024/143](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2024/1729594417893.pdf) of {{date:2024-10-22}} bars exchanges, brokers, fund houses and the people who act for them from associating with anyone who gives securities advice without registration or makes claims about returns. The circular carves out people engaged in investor education who do neither. This site takes no money from any intermediary and has no paid tips product, no Telegram or WhatsApp channel and no “sure-shot” anything.
- **The thirty-day rule.** [SEBI circular CIR/2025/11](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2026/1784884437684.pdf) of {{date:2025-01-29}}, updated on {{date:2026-05-08}}, explains how SEBI reads “education”. An educator may not use market price data from the preceding thirty days to name a security, by name, ticker, code name or screenshot, in a way that suggests where its price is going or that it should be bought or sold. The guides here therefore explain mechanisms with historical or hypothetical numbers and never pair a named security with a view on where its price is going.

In plain terms: the site explains how things work and cites the rule or the study, and it stops where a recommendation would begin.

## How to check anyone who sells advice

Before paying for calls, research or advice, look the person or firm up on SEBI’s public lists of [registered investment advisers](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=13) and [registered research analysts](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/other/OtherAction.do?doRecognisedFpi=yes&intmId=14); a registration number quoted in a message can be checked there in a minute. Complaints against a registered intermediary or a listed company go to [SCORES](https://scores.sebi.gov.in/), and disputes with one can be taken to the [SMART ODR](https://smartodr.in/) online dispute-resolution portal. SEBI’s own [investor website](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/) carries its awareness material. The site’s page of [data sites, broker APIs and communities](../resources/) links each of these as well.

## Corrections

Rules change and mistakes happen. If a figure is stale, a rule has moved or a source is misread, [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/Sudish007/sauda-shastra/issues) with the page address and the document that shows the correction; fixes are recorded on [What changed](../changelog/) when they alter a rule the guides state. The [glossary](../glossary/) and the [facts register](../facts/) are the quickest places to see what the site currently believes a term or a number to be.
